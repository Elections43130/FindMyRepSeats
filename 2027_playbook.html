2027 Local Playbook

In 2027, people in Fairfield County will vote for their city and village leaders. This page shows every one of those jobs. It shows who won last time, how many votes they got, and how many Democrats in that town usually vote. Look at the blue cards first. Those are seats where not enough people ran. Some of them had nobody at all.

What the words on this page mean

Empty seats
Not enough people ran last time. Some seats had nobody at all. If you sign up for one of these and nobody else does, you win.
No race
Only one person ran for each seat, so they won without a fight.
Real race
More people ran than there were seats.
Votes you need to win
Our best guess of how many votes it takes, based on last time.
Democrats here who voted
Democrats in this town who already vote in these smaller odd-year elections. They are your starting team.
Turn-out Democrats
Democrats who voted for president in 2024 but stayed home in the last local election. They already lean our way. They just need a reminder that there is an election and why it matters.
Voters who do not pick a party
People who have never voted in a Democratic or Republican primary. Many of them will vote for a good neighbor no matter the party.

How to read the map

Gray: empty seats. Not enough people ran last time. Sign up and you can win.
Red: a Republican holds it. These races list your party on the ballot (Lancaster).
Blue: a Democrat holds it. Also a party race. (None right now. Let us change that.)
Light blue: an independent holds it. A party race where someone beat the Republican without one.
Purple: no party on the ballot. Villages and Pickerington. People vote for the person, not the party.
Light gray: no city or village race here in 2027 (townships), or a town mostly in another county.

Lancaster is colored by who holds each ward's council seat. Lancaster's mayor, auditor, law director, council president and 4 at-large seats are all Republican and had no race last time. Tap any town to see its jobs below.

How we got these numbers
1. Election results come from the Fairfield County Board of Elections for 2021, 2023 and 2025. Voter counts come from the county voter list.
2. "Last time" means the last election for the same seats. Lancaster council and president of council serve 2 years, so we used 2025. Mayors, the other Lancaster city offices, Pickerington council and village councils serve 4 years, so we used 2023.
3. Empty seats: if the number of people on the ballot is the same as or less than the number of seats, everyone on the ballot wins. Someone could still sign up as a write-in candidate, so check with the Board of Elections close to the deadline. "Votes you need to win": if there is 1 seat, it is half of the people who voted last time, plus 1. If there are several seats, it is 1 more vote than the person who won the last seat.
4. "Democrats here who voted" means people whose last primary ballot was a Democratic one and who voted in that odd year. People who never pick a party are not counted there. Most of these races do not list a party on the ballot, so being a good neighbor matters more than party.
5. "Turn-out Democrats" voted in the 2024 election for president but did not vote in that odd year.
6. Lots of extra people voted in 2023 because of the abortion and marijuana questions. So the 2023 numbers may be higher than 2027 will be.
7. Always check with the Board of Elections how many seats are open and how long the terms are before someone signs up.
8. Canal Winchester, Columbus, Reynoldsburg and Buckeye Lake are left out. Most of those towns are in other counties, so our numbers would be missing most of their voters.
