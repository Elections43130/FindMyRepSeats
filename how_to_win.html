How to Win

You've decided to run. Now what? The less flashy races don't come with a campaign team. It's you, maybe a friend, going door to door and hoping what you're doing is the right thing to win. This is that guide: your guide to the numbers in your race, and your guide to getting to the finish line.

Pick a race to see how many votes it takes, where to find them, what to say, and when to do it. Each one is an honest take on whether the seat is winnable this cycle or needs someone to pull it closer for next time. 2027 is an off year with lower turnout, so those seats can be won by talking to your neighbors and showing up at a few events. 2028 is a presidential year with a bigger gap to close, so it takes getting out of your bubble. Each race is what you make it.

  • 963 people who usually vote in Republican primaries. In 2023, 51% of voters here said Yes to abortion rights, so many Republicans here already agree with us on big issues.

Your calendar

  1. Now to JanuaryDecide to run. Tell your family. Pick a treasurer (a person who tracks the money). Talk to the county Democratic Party.

  5. May 4, 2027Primary election. If no other Democrat files, you are on the November ballot.

  7. October 4Last day to register to vote. Help new neighbors sign up before this.
  8. October 5Early voting starts. Remind your Democrats to vote early.
  9. November 2, 2027Election Day.

Missed February 3? You can still run as an independent. That deadline is May 3, 2027, and needs more signatures.

Who to talk to

What to say

  • Talk about the town: roads, water bills, parks, safety, how the council spends money. Local races are won on local issues.
