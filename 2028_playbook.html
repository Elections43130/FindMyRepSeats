2028 Neighborhood Playbook

A precinct is a small voting neighborhood. Fairfield County has 111 of them. This page makes a guess about how each one could vote in 2028. How to use it: 1. Pick a race. 2. Pick how much of our 2026 gains we keep. 3. Start at the top of the list. The top rows are the closest races, so they are the best places to work first. Lower down are places we are winning by a little, which we need to protect.

How we got these numbers
1. This is a guess, not a promise. Nobody knows exactly what will happen in 2028.
2. For each precinct we start with how the Democrat did in 2024. Then we add part of how much better the Democrat did in 2026. The slider picks how much of that 2026 gain we keep.
3. We assume about as many people vote as in 2024, when people voted for president.
4. "Behind by" is how many more votes the Republican would get. "Ahead by" means we would get more.
5. "Change vote" counts people who voted Republican and switch to us. Each one counts twice: one less vote for them, one more for us. So you need half as many of these as new voters.
6. "Or new voters" counts Democrats who did not vote before and now do.
7. Races used: President uses Harris in 2024 and Acton in 2026. U.S. Senate uses Brown both years. OH-12 uses Christian both years. Ohio House 73 uses Scarmack in 2024 and Wood in 2026. Ohio House 69 uses Hough in 2024 and Rigsby in 2026.
8. The Senate and OH-12 races also happen outside our county. This page only shows Fairfield County.
9. "Abortion Yes 2023" is how much of the precinct voted Yes on State Issue 1 in November 2023. Issue 1 put abortion rights in the Ohio Constitution. Issue 2 made marijuana legal. Both passed in Fairfield County: Issue 1 got 51.4% Yes and Issue 2 got 52.6% Yes.
10. "Extra if we match" is a rough count of people who voted Yes on abortion rights but would not vote for our candidate. These people already agree with us on a big issue, so they are good people to talk to. It uses the 2028 guess for how many people vote. The 2023 voters were not exactly the same people, so this is a guide, not an exact count.
11. Election results come from the Fairfield County Board of Elections. The precinct lines on the map are close but not exact.
